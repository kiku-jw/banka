# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Russian-speaking Jehovah's Witness friends play together in one room or over
video. One host keeps the game visible, enters the names, and moves through the
turns. The group may include two people or a larger company with different
backgrounds and levels of ministry experience.

## Product Purpose

«Banka!» helps friends relax, laugh, and learn something real about one
another through a jar of conversational questions and small activities. Success
means the interface disappears into the evening: questions sound natural aloud,
the host never has to explain the mechanics, and nobody is pressured to provide
an answer or experience they do not have.

## Brand Personality

Warm, playful, sincere. The game should feel tactile and memorable without
becoming childish, theatrical, sentimental, or artificially profound.

## Anti-references

- AI-written interview prompts, therapy language, abstract self-analysis, and
  visible difficulty or category systems.
- Literal question illustrations that dictate an answer, abstract images added
  merely for mood, or any image that requires players to inspect it.
- Orthodox, church, national, denominational, or invented religious imagery.
- Competitive party-game scoring, loud casino feedback, or long decorative
  sequences that interrupt conversation.

## Design Principles

1. Conversation remains the main event; the interface creates rhythm and then
   gets out of the way.
2. Delight is tactile, brief, and varied: paper, glass, light, quiet sound, and
   occasional visual surprises.
3. Every prompt works for two people or a larger group, in person or over video.
4. An optional image appears only when several concrete objects or places can
   help someone find an answer without narrowing it to one pictured option.
5. Personal spiritual stories stay sincere and visually unmanipulated.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

The game remains operable by keyboard and readable on phones, laptops, shared
screens, and televisions. Text stays HTML rather than being baked into media.
Motion can be disabled, `prefers-reduced-motion` is respected, sound and music
are independent, color is not the sole carrier of meaning, and optional
answer-idea images are hidden from assistive technology.
